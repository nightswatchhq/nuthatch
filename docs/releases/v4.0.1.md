Nuthatch 4.0.1 replaces the two public Ethereum mainnet endpoints a new nest is given, because neither
could serve a backfill. It is 4.0.0 plus that one change (#1614), cut from the `release/4.0` branch
rather than from `main`, which is how every 4.0.x patch will be cut. Anyone who already runs a mainnet
nest on their own RPC has nothing to do.

## Compatibility and upgrade

Replace the binary and restart. There is no storage migration, configuration change, NID change or
re-index.

**An existing nest is not changed.** `init` writes the default endpoints into `nuthatch.toml`, and a
nest keeps what it was given. A mainnet nest created by 4.0.0 or earlier still lists
`eth-pokt.nodies.app` and `eth.drpc.org`. To move it, pass `--rpc`, which replaces the pool at runtime
and leaves the nest's identity alone. Editing `rpc_urls` in `nuthatch.toml` also works but gives the
nest a different content address (`nuthatch nest nid`), as editing that file always has.

## Mainnet defaults that serve history (#1614)

`eth.drpc.org` no longer keeps history: every `eth_getLogs` below its pruning horizon is HTTP 400
"Unknown state". `eth-pokt.nodies.app` keeps history but caps `eth_getLogs` at 50 blocks and throttles.
On that pair, `nuthatch dev` on USDC from its deployment block had 0 rows after 122 seconds, which is
#1607; 4.0.0 worked round it by starting the README demo near the tip.

A new mainnet nest now gets `mainnet.gateway.tenderly.co` and `rpc.mevblocker.io`. They are the two of
twenty keyless endpoints that passed `nuthatch doctor` on 1 October 2026 and then answered
`eth_getLogs` at blocks 6.5 million and 12 million, a depth doctor's own archive probe does not reach.
On them, USDC from deployment had 126,362 rows after 121 seconds with no retries. That is one run from
one machine, over the sparse early blocks, and is not a figure for the whole history.

Public endpoints are a measurement with a date on it. `nuthatch doctor --rpc <url>` is how to check
one before trusting a long backfill to it, ours included.
