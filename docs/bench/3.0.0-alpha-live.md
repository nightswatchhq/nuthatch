# 3.0.0-alpha: the numbers

The four items in `docs/releases/3.0.0-alpha-stresstest.md`, and where each stands. Numbers from runs
someone can point at, per that document's own bar - not "the tests pass", which they already did
while three of the four defects below were live.

Hardware: a 32-core Debian box (the ThinkPad) unless stated. Corpora live outside the repo; the
probes that produce these figures skip cleanly without them.

## Item 1 - what a warm-restart seed costs. **Answered.**

`tests/seed_scale.rs`, against a copy of the real Lodestar nest and a larger Horizon corpus.

| corpus | sealed rows | segments | seed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lodestar `service__indexing_rewards_collected` | 249,979 | 733 | **1.9 s** |
| Horizon `staking_legacy__stake_delegated` | 346,288 | 2,985 | **2.4 s** |

So adding an `entities.toml` to a nest with history costs about two seconds on its next restart, per
entity. That is a figure rather than a warning, which is what item 1 was for.

Seeding streams one sealed segment at a time rather than materialising the whole history: peak RSS
993 MB against 694 MB on the single table, 790 MB against 368 MB on a joined entity, for roughly
twice the wall clock on a path that runs once per restart.

**This item found #894**, and would not have been found any other way: fed as one window, a seed kept
only `groups mod 10,000` of the relation. 309,549 distinct keys in, 9,549 groups out, nothing faulted
and nothing logged.

## Item 2 - whether an entity survives live tip-following. **In progress, 14 h of 48 h.**

A USDC nest on Arbitrum through Alchemy, `GROUP BY to SUM(value)`, one sample a minute.

| | |
|---|---|
| samples | 835 over 14.0 h |
| samples where the entity was behind the indexed head | **0** |
| samples where `current` was not true | **0** |
| nest tip lag, blocks | p50 **0**, p99 9, max 10 |
| relation | 3,097 → **62,787** rows |

An entity kept up with every block on a 0.25-second chain for fourteen hours while its relation grew
twentyfold. The nest's own tip lag never exceeded ten blocks, and that is the nest against the chain,
not the entity against the nest.

**This item found #903.** A sibling control nest died 3.6 hours in: a tip race, where the provider
answers `block range extends beyond current head block`, matched the getLogs cap marker list and was
narrowed to a single block before the loop gave up with a diagnosis that was never true. Not
entity-related - it would kill any nest at tip. Fixed in #904, and the restarted control carries it.

**What this run does not test: reorg at tip.** `nuthatch_nest_reorgs_total` reads **0** after fifteen
hours, and the nest log has never used the word. Arbitrum is a sequencer chain and does not reorg at
the depth a nest follows, so no retraction has been exercised live however long this runs.

Retraction is not untested - `an_entity_converges_at_any_reorg_depth` drives randomised reorg depths
through `spawn_nest` and the real ingest path, and #822 criterion 12 checks the reorged relation
against the authored SQL it replaces. But those are fixtures, and the whole argument for a live run
is that fixtures did not find #894, #897 or #903.

## Item 2b - a real reorg, on a chain that has them. **Happened.**

So a third nest was added on Ethereum mainnet, same entity shape, 12-second blocks and therefore
about a fortieth of Arbitrum's call rate. Three hours in:

```
2026-08-28T12:58:22Z WARN reorg detected: rolled back to block 25853886 (removed 32 entities)
```

Thirty-two rows retracted. Immediately afterwards the entity read `applied_through` 25,853,958,
`current: true`, `faulted: false`, `wedged: false`, 36,858 rows - it took the retraction and carried
on.

**What that proves and what it does not.** It proves the mechanism survives a real reorg on a live
chain: the rollback reached the circuit, the retraction was folded, and nothing faulted or wedged. It
does **not** prove the relation converged to the right answer, and no live run can - there is no
clean replay to compare against, which is exactly what the property test exists to do. The honest
claim is "a real reorg was handled without fault or wedge", and the correctness claim stays with
criterion 12.

## Item 3 - a restart after days of uptime. **Not started.**

Waiting on item 2 to accumulate the uptime. The one restart so far was the control coming back after
#904, which caught up 134,000 blocks in about two minutes - not the test, which is about a process
that has been *up* for days rather than one that has been *down*.

## Item 4 - whether the two admission constants hold. **Half answered, and the other half is not yet measurable.**

`ENTITY_RSS_BYTES_PER_ROW = 3,200` was derived from an 889-row corpus, where fixed allocations
dominate. At real scale it is **940 bytes/row** for a one-column key and one aggregate, and **1,482**
for two key columns and three aggregates, over ~310,000 maintained rows. So the constant is between
2.2x and 3.4x the amortised truth, in the safe direction. Left unchanged deliberately: 1,482 is the
widest shape measured rather than the widest possible.

The live figure is **not yet attributable and should not be quoted.** A control nest indexes the same
contract from the same block with no entity declared, and the difference between the two processes is
meant to be the entity. It is not, yet: the two started 27 minutes apart, then the control died and
restarted 13 hours later, so at any point one of them has been climbing its own allocation curve. An
early read gave "2.7 kB per maintained row, falling" - an artefact of two processes at different ages,
and seven times the static-corpus figure. The number becomes real when both have been up long enough
for that to wash out.

## What the criteria runs produced

Not items 1-4, but measured against the same real data and worth keeping beside them. From #822's
criteria 3 and 4, on the Lodestar `indexer_rewards` panel:

| | p50 | p99 |
|---|---|---|
| authored view, recomputed per query | 2.15 s | 2.20 s |
| maintained relation | **87.7 ms** | **92.1 ms** |

Identical results - 82 rows each. And one block's update against a relation of 309,548 groups is
**285 µs**, against 195 µs at 61 groups: flat, which is criterion 5's whole claim and was **failing**
at 72 ms when first measured (#897).
